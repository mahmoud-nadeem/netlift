# Stakeholder analysis

Author: Mohammed Sherif · Task: T31

NetLift has no live business users this semester: both datasets are historical, public and de-identified, and the "campaign" is a simulated replay over a held-out split. The five business stakeholders below are therefore the people NetLift is designed for, represented by personas, while the DEPI instructor and evaluators are the only stakeholders who act on the project today. Capabilities under "How NetLift meets them" are planned unless stated otherwise; the task numbers and due dates come from `docs/project-plan.md` (revised 13 October 2026), and a capability counts as implemented only once its code is merged into `main`, its tests pass in CI, and it has been checked against the requirement it serves; merged alone is not enough. Repository state was checked by the author on 15 October 2026 with `git grep`, `git ls-files` and `git ls-tree` against `main` (commit 22062c4) and `origin/t36-interfaces`; those commands show which files and symbols exist, not that the code works. The exact commands are listed under Sources. Where code exists only on the T36 branch, the text says so. Stakeholder names follow the actor names in the T32 draft (its question Q6), and use cases are cited by name, as in that draft (PR #44, which was under review when the author last read it), because its IDs may change. **Influence** is the team's own assessment of how much a stakeholder decides whether the system is used or accepted; for the business roles it is their influence in a real deployment.

| Marketing Analyst (retention / CRM) | Selects a customer base, sets a budget and a per-contact cost, and hands a target list to campaign operations. | A target list that spends the budget on customers whose behaviour changes, not on customers who would have converted anyway. | Medium | A ranked list with an expected incremental-conversion figure and an uncertainty range; a clear contact / suppress / no-action recommendation; a clear "we do not know" state. | Planned (T32 use cases: define a replay scenario, score a batch, allocate under budget, inspect a customer explanation): the scoring response (`uplift_score`, `uplift_ci_95`, `recommendation` and a separate `verdict`) is frozen in T36 (due 20 Oct); on `main` the API package contains only `__init__.py`, and a `schema.py` exists on branch `t36-interfaces` (it defines `model_version`, `uplift_ci_95`, `recommendation` and `verdict` as separate fields; only lines matching those names were read), and the FastAPI service is T59 (due 20 Nov); no dashboard files exist on `main` or on `t36-interfaces`, and the planner tab is part of the dashboard, T62 (due 27 Nov). | Campaign operations | Executes the contact list through a campaign tool and keeps the records. | A list they can act on without interpreting model output, and a record of who was contacted and who was held back. | Medium | An unambiguous action per customer; an explicit difference between "do not contact" and "we do not know"; assignment recorded at the time it is made. | Planned (T32 use case: deliver the target list to a campaign): `recommendation` and `verdict` are separate fields in the frozen scoring response (T36), and the dashboard (T62) is to render an empty recommendation differently from a confident one. | Finance / Campaign Audit | Reads the incremental-profit report and the evidence behind it. | A number they can audit, with the assumptions and the control group that produced it. | High | Incremental profit per unit of spend with an interval and the cost and margin named beside it; the number of control customers and control conversions behind each figure; the same figure reproducible from the repository. | Planned (T32 user story: audit a profit figure): replay output with incremental profit per unit of spend (K3, simulator T61) reported with intervals and stated assumptions, and a one-command reproduction of the headline result (T67). We also flag how few control conversions sit behind each decile: about 406 on average. This is an estimate calculated from summary figures, not an observed per-decile count: the specification gives 2,096,937 control rows (13,979,592 minus 11,882,655 treated) and 4,063 control conversions in total at the measured control rate of 0.1938% (T16 verification), and 4,063 divided by ten deciles is about 406. Real per-decile counts will differ with the scores and have not been measured yet (the harness, T39, will count them); they are why intervals are mandatory. The specification's earlier figure of about 600 used the published overall rate and was an overestimate. | "Saved the company $X" (write "estimated a $X incremental-profit opportunity at the stated contact cost and margin, measured on held-out experimental data"); a point estimate without its interval; that profit is realised rather than estimated. |
| Customers whose data is scored | The people behind the rows; they are never contacted by this project. | That they are not harmed by being scored, profiled or treated differently. | Low (highest stake) | No personal data processed; no manipulative or vulnerable-customer targeting; no unexamined discrimination between segments; abstention instead of false confidence. | The specification describes both datasets as public and de-identified, and the T17 note records that neither file has an ID column; we did not test re-identification risk ourselves. The T16 note states that raw files are not committed (loader scripts and checksums are), and `git ls-files data` in the author's working copy lists only three `.gitkeep` files under `data/`; whether `.gitignore` also covers raw files was not confirmed. The specification calls for the report to state a scope of acceptable use, to name and justify the control-group holdout, and to report targeting rates across the available segments. | That real individuals were scored or contacted; that the system has been checked for fairness beyond what anonymised Criteo features allow (the segment check is structural only, and the report says so); that the method is safe for targeting vulnerable customers. |

## Needs met this semester, and needs that belong to a real deployment

"In scope this semester" means the need is in scope for the deliverables, not that it is met today. Rows that depend on code (scoring, dashboard, simulator, tracking) are planned unless their Why cell says otherwise, and become met only when the code is merged, tested and verified.

| Need | In scope this semester | Deployment only | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ranked target list with uplift interval, for a given budget, cost and margin | yes | no | Planned: scoring API (T59) and planner tab (T62) run on the replay scenario over the held-out split. |
| Compare uplift, response model, random and blanket at equal budget, with intervals | yes | no | This is K1 (T44) and the replay simulator (T61). |
| Abstention verdicts distinct from a confident recommendation | yes | no | Two separate things, both planned. The verdict field in the scoring response is specified in T36 (branch `t36-interfaces`, not merged; it defines `verdict` with the three values and its validation rejects any non-`confident` verdict that carries `contact` or `suppress`). Displaying an abstention differently from a confident result is dashboard behaviour: guidelines in T49 (4 Nov), implementation in T62 (27 Nov); no dashboard code exists. |
| Model version recorded on every score; reproducible headline result | yes | no | `model_version` is in the output schema (T36, on branch `t36-interfaces`, not yet merged); a tracking wrapper is on `main` and the MLflow harness is T41; the K8 reproducibility check is T67. |
| Incremental result measured against a control group, with its size reported | yes | no | The replay uses the control arm of the recorded experiment. |
