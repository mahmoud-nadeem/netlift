# Stakeholder analysis

Author: Mohammed Sherif · Task: T31

NetLift has no live business users this semester: both datasets are historical, public and de-identified, and the "campaign" is a simulated replay over a held-out split. The five business stakeholders below are therefore the people NetLift is designed for, represented by personas, while the DEPI instructor and evaluators are the only stakeholders who act on the project today. Capabilities under "How NetLift meets them" are planned unless stated otherwise; the task numbers and due dates come from `docs/project-plan.md` (revised 13 October 2026), and a capability counts as implemented only once its pull request is merged. **Influence** is the team's own assessment of how much a stakeholder decides whether the system is used or accepted; for the business roles it is their influence in a real deployment.

## Stakeholders

| Stakeholder | Role | Interest in NetLift | Influence | Needs | How NetLift meets them | What we must not claim to them |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Marketing analyst (retention / CRM) | Selects a customer base, sets a budget and a per-contact cost, and hands a target list to campaign operations. | A target list that spends the budget on customers whose behaviour changes, not on customers who would have converted anyway. | Medium | A ranked list with an expected incremental-conversion figure and an uncertainty range; a clear contact / suppress / no-action recommendation; a clear "we do not know" state. | Planned: the scoring response (`uplift_score`, `uplift_ci_95`, `recommendation` and a separate `verdict`) is frozen in T36 (due 20 Oct); the FastAPI service is T59 (due 20 Nov) and the planner tab is part of the dashboard, T62 (due 27 Nov). The two abstention verdicts (`insufficient_evidence`, `insufficient_control_data`) are to be rendered visually distinct from a confident result, as the UI/UX guidelines (T49, due 4 Nov) will require. | That the figure is realised money (it is estimated at stated cost and margin on held-out experimental data); that the replay is a live campaign; that the effect applies to customers who actually saw the offer (the MVP estimates the effect of being selected, ITT); any accuracy or F1 figure. |
| Marketing manager | Decides whether to commit spend, comparing candidate policies. | Evidence that the uplift policy is worth running instead of the response-model policy, at the same budget. | High | One screen comparing uplift, response model, random and blanket contact at equal budget, with intervals; the break-even ratio (cost / margin) exposed as an input. | Planned: the Policy Comparison tab (T62, due 27 Nov), fed by the campaign policy simulator (T61, due 20 Nov), with K1 as the central comparison (T44, due 6 Nov); the cost and margin assumptions are to appear on the same line as the result. | That uplift beats the response model before K1 is measured: an interval that contains zero is a reportable finding, not a failure; that a customer on the suppression list is a "sleeping dog" (we estimate a confidently negative effect for a subgroup; no dataset labels individuals). |
| Data scientist | Consumes the scoring API, inspects model evidence, retrains. | Reproducible, inspectable results and a fair model comparison. | Medium | Single and batch scoring with the model version recorded on every score; Qini curves and metrics with bootstrap intervals; tracked, reproducible runs; tests that fail when a forbidden column reaches the model matrix. | Planned: `model_version` in every scoring response (schema T36, due 20 Oct; FastAPI service T59, due 20 Nov); an evaluation harness with Qini, AUUC, uplift@k and bootstrap intervals within arms (T39, due 30 Oct); MLflow tracking that records the commit, dataset hash and seed (T41, due 30 Oct); `validate_features()` rejecting forbidden columns by name, with a unit test asserting that it fails (pipeline T37, due 27 Oct; CI enforcement T58, due 20 Nov); a one-command reproduction of the headline result for K8 (T67, due 30 Nov). | That any model is "the best" without a measured interval on the locked test split; that the neural estimator was favoured (it is a challenger, and the tree baseline may win); that `segment_label` is ground truth (it is derived); that results hold on non-experimental data; that the test split was used for tuning. |
| Campaign operations | Executes the contact list through a campaign tool and keeps the records. | A list they can act on without interpreting model output, and a record of who was contacted and who was held back. | Medium | An unambiguous action per customer; an explicit difference between "do not contact" and "we do not know"; assignment recorded at the time it is made. | Planned: `recommendation` and `verdict` are separate fields in the frozen scoring response (T36), and the dashboard (T62) is to render an empty recommendation differently from a confident one. The Campaign Execution System is drawn in the use case diagram (T46) as an external, deployment-only actor. | That NetLift sends or executes campaigns; that `no_action` or an abstention verdict means "suppress"; that treatment assignment is being recorded for a live campaign. |
| Finance / audit | Reads the incremental-profit report and the evidence behind it. | A number they can audit, with the assumptions and the control group that produced it. | High | Incremental profit per unit of spend with an interval and the cost and margin named beside it; the number of control customers and control conversions behind each figure; the same figure reproducible from the repository. | Planned: replay output with incremental profit per unit of spend (K3, simulator T61) reported with intervals and stated assumptions, and a one-command reproduction of the headline result (T67). We also flag how few control conversions sit behind each decile: about 406 on average if they spread evenly. That follows from the counted totals in the specification (2,096,937 control rows, 4,063 control conversions at 0.1938%, so about 210,000 control rows per decile). The 406 is an even-spread average, not a per-decile count, and the deciles that matter most will hold fewer; this is why intervals are mandatory. | "Saved the company $X" (write "estimated a $X incremental-profit opportunity at the stated contact cost and margin, measured on held-out experimental data"); a point estimate without its interval; that profit is realised rather than estimated. |
| Customers whose data is scored | The people behind the rows; they are never contacted by this project. | That they are not harmed by being scored, profiled or treated differently. | Low (highest stake) | No personal data processed; no manipulative or vulnerable-customer targeting; no unexamined discrimination between segments; abstention instead of false confidence. | Both datasets are public and de-identified, and `.gitignore` excludes raw data (`data/raw/*`, keeping only `data/raw/.gitkeep`), so the repository holds loader scripts and checksums rather than data. The scope of acceptable use is stated in the final report (T69, Mahmoud); the targeting-rate check across Hillstrom segments is a table in the error analysis (T65, Ali), and it is structural only on Criteo. The control-group holdout applies to a real deployment: in the replay nobody is withheld, because the control group is already in the data. | That real individuals were scored or contacted; that the system has been checked for fairness beyond what anonymised Criteo features allow (the segment check is structural only, and the report says so); that the method is safe for targeting vulnerable customers. |
| DEPI instructor and evaluators | Assess the project against the DEPI documentation guidelines and the final evaluation. | A complete, honest project in which each member's contribution can be shown. | High | Every required artefact (use cases, requirements, architecture, ERD, DFD, sequence / activity / state / class diagrams, wireframes, UI/UX guidelines, API documentation, test plan); evidence of what each member built; scope and limitations stated rather than hidden. | The documentation set built across the milestones; the GitHub record (issue assignees, pull-request authors, reviewers) as evidence of individual work; computer vision and NLP stated as absent, with the instructor's review recording that they are not required. | That the replay is a live campaign; that anything in the deployment-only list was built or exercised; that any metric is valid without an interval; that CV or NLP are covered; that a figure was verified against the downloaded files when it was taken from documentation. |

## Needs met this semester, and needs that belong to a real deployment

"This semester" means the need is in scope for the deliverables and is met once the matching deliverable passes review.

| Need | This semester | Deployment only | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ranked target list with uplift interval, for a given budget, cost and margin | yes | no | Planned: scoring API (T59) and planner tab (T62) run on the replay scenario over the held-out split. |
| Compare uplift, response model, random and blanket at equal budget, with intervals | yes | no | This is K1 (T44) and the replay simulator (T61). |
| Abstention verdicts distinct from a confident recommendation | yes | no | A functional and non-functional requirement; no recommendation is issued without a verdict field. |
| Model version recorded on every score; reproducible headline result | yes | no | `model_version` is in the output schema (T36, due 20 Oct); MLflow tracking is T41 (due 30 Oct); the K8 reproducibility check is T67. |
| Incremental result measured against a control group, with its size reported | yes | no | The replay uses the control arm of the recorded experiment. |
| Permanently maintained randomized holdout for live campaigns | no | yes | In production the counterfactual has to be manufactured by withholding contact from a randomized share; it is designed for, not exercised. |
| Realised (not estimated) incremental profit | no | yes | Needs a real outcome window and real spend; this semester only produces an estimate under assumed cost and margin. |
| Integration with a campaign execution system; assignment recorded at assignment time | no | yes | The Campaign Execution System is an external, deployment-only actor. |
| Retraining run | yes | no | An offline pipeline job, in scope this semester (ADR 0002, PR #95). |
| Scheduled retraining trigger (drift) | no | yes | Deployment-only (ADR 0002, PR #95). |
| No personal data processed | yes | no | Both datasets are public and de-identified. |
| Handling real customers' personal data (consent, access control, retention) | no | yes | Not exercised; the project processes no personal data. |
| Scope of acceptable use | yes | no | Stated in the final report (T69). |
| Segment targeting-rate check | yes | no | A table in the error analysis (T65); on Criteo it is structural only because the features are anonymised. |
| Required DEPI artefacts and per-member evidence of contribution | yes | no | Produced across the milestones; the GitHub record is the evidence. |
